## Verdict

**ACCEPTED AFTER ONE ROUND.** The reviewer did not accept the branch. It returned 37 findings, of which three were `high` and two of those were crash routes, and the branch was repaired rather than argued with.

The finding worth naming is 3, and it is the reason this record exists rather than a bare list. R-032's mitigation contained the sentence "checked with a jar, not assumed". It was false, and it was the specific sentence that stopped anyone from checking — the gate excluded `oq-core` because a document said the exclusion had been verified, and behind the exclusion were two paths where a program's own text crashed the interpreter with a message blaming the interpreter. A false evidence claim is worse than a missing one, because a missing one invites the check that a false one forecloses.

The second-order pattern is finding 14 and finding 9 together. Every count of the builtins that reach a path parse was wrong in a different direction — 16, 15, 14 — and each was written by someone who had just changed the thing being counted. The resolution was not to pick a better number but to run a jar: 29 builtins, 23 leaking on `main`, 0 on this branch. And the mutation test for the gate exists because a gate whose tests pass when the gate is disabled is a gate with no evidence, which is the same failure in a different file.

Nine of the 37 are documentation figures, and six of those are the same two numbers (`17` targets, `29` builtins) stated wrongly in different places. `check-report-claims.py` checks the figures it knows about and passed throughout, because it does not know about these. That is a limit of the gate and not a defect in this change, and it is recorded here rather than repaired.

## What this review does not establish

- **It is not independent.** Same model, same endpoint. One round of a reviewer sharing the implementer's blind spots is one blind spot examined twice. The findings it returned are real and were repaired, which is evidence the mechanism works; it is not evidence that a second round would find nothing.
- **It does not establish that 37 is the count.** The reviewer was instructed to falsify claims against the tree and did, but the tree is large and the instruction was not exhaustive. A second reviewer would plausibly find findings this one did not.
- **It does not establish that the gate is complete.** The gate proves no `Path.of`/`Paths.get` appears outside seven named sites in three modules. It cannot prove that a new *different* way to parse a path — `FileSystems.getPath`, `URI`, a `new File(...)` — would be caught. Finding 6 was about exactly that class of evasion and was repaired for the spellings the reviewer knew; the next spelling is not in the corpus.
- **It does not establish that the seven allowed sites are all necessary.** Each has a written reason and four are `Path.of(".")` defaults. Whether those four should share a helper is a design question this review did not test.
- **It does not establish that the message shape is right.** It establishes that 29 builtins now give one shape and that a test pins it. Whether `read() needs a path, and 'x' is not one` is the best available wording is a judgement about diagnostics, not a fact about correctness, and no finding addressed it.
- **The category verdicts are wrong as a summary.** Every category reads `pass` on a branch that carried two `high` crash findings. The table records the branch after repair, which is the only state the document can be checked against, but a reader who reads only the table would not learn that the branch ever crashed. That is the gap named above and it is not repaired by this record.
