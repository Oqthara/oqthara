## What this round was worth

The change adds one document and one function, and the document is the reason the function exists.

Every row was executed against the CLI built from `d7d3c05`, never read out of the source. That
distinction found five of the six findings that matter. Reading `Values.java` would have shown a
`+` branch that handles strings; running `1 + "a"` and getting `1a` shows a coercion rule the spec
never states and no conformance case ever exercises. Reading `ListOps` would have shown a `sort`;
running it twice shows that `.sort()` leaves its receiver alone while every sibling method on the
same list mutates.

It also lost three findings that a reader would have kept, and losing them is the more useful
result. `-2 ** 2` returning `4` looks like a precedence bug until `docs/spec/language.md:319` says
it is a decision, published from the parser. Both tuple-assignment refusals look like missing
destructuring until spec:60–92 says the refusal is the design. The ten leader words look absent
from the editor grammar until spec:1260 says they are names. An audit that reported those three as
defects would have been more confident and less correct.

The arithmetic is the part I would not defend from memory. Four hand-counts gave four answers, and
the correction each time came from a script rather than from rereading. So the derivation is now
the gate: `check_capability_matrix` reads the table and compares against both places the document
restates it. The first two versions of that gate were themselves wrong — one matched nothing, one
read `L3` as a count from a commentary line — which is the same failure in a different medium and
is why the paragraph is now sliced before it is searched.

The seven tests that cover it are the smallest part of the change and the part most likely to
outlive the others. They pass strings to the checker rather than editing the document, because the
first draft wrote to a tracked file and restored it in a `finally`, which is one interrupted run
away from committing a mutated matrix.

## What this review does not claim

It does not claim the matrix is complete. It covers the nine areas §3 names at the granularity one
probe per row can establish; a narrower question inside any row may still be unanswered, and the
document says so in its own disclaimers rather than only here.

It does not claim any verdict is final. A row marked Complete is complete as of `d7d3c05`; if a
later change breaks it, nothing in this PR notices. The gate checks the arithmetic, not the
language.

It does not claim the five actionable findings are the important five. They are the five that were
both reproducible and cheap to describe. Two rows (L4.9, L4.8) are recorded and deliberately not
actioned, and one (L8.3) was recorded Complete on inspection — a reader who disagrees with any of
those three judgments is disagreeing with a stated position rather than finding an omission.

It does not claim independent review. The same session wrote the change and audited it, which
ADR-0059 requires be stated rather than glossed.
