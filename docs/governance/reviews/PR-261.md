## What this round was worth

Item 9b sampled four claims and found three stale. The obvious inference was that more were stale,
so the useful move was to stop sampling and re-derive everything quantitative.

The result is more informative than "five more were wrong". **Item 8 held up completely** —
thirteen directions, 52 capability rows, D1–D5, 19 probes over C1–C8, 69 ADRs — and it is the one
item whose cited documents nobody has edited since the report was written. Every item that decayed
is one whose subject later commits touched. That is a mechanism, not a rate: a claim survives
exactly as long as nobody works on what it describes.

Which makes the sharpest finding uncomfortable in a specific way. Four of the five stale claims
were invalidated by commits executing the report's **own** Item 9 sequence — PR #252 is entry 2,
PR #253 is entry 3, PR #254 is entry 1, and ADR-0069 is entry 4. The report decayed because it
worked. A governance document that orders its remediation will be contradicted by that remediation,
and no amount of care at authoring time changes that. The only thing that would is re-deriving the
claims on a schedule, and nothing does.

The severity gradient inside those five is the part that justifies the audit. Four were counts. One
— Item 7 — was a conclusion, and it said §5 was two supersessions from unblocked when the
supersessions had already happened against it. A reader planning §5 from this report would have
written a record reproducing a decision that already existed. PR #260 caught the version of that
mistake in Item 10 and left the root of it in Item 7, which is what a report contradicts itself
looks like from the inside: the correction landed where the claim was noticed, not where it started.

Two findings are worth calling out because they were mine and I caught them mid-branch. Finding 7 —
I attributed the `Low` bucket's rise to R-030, which was already present, instead of to R-018's
exposure change. Finding 8 — I asserted ADR-0069 spent the reversibility it inherited, when the ADR
says in terms that reopening is one decision. Both were inferences that felt supported by having
read the surrounding material, and both were wrong in a way one `git show` or one `sed -n` settled.
That is the same failure the report is about, arriving in the correction to it.

  front of it.

  the status table despite §4 having no definition anywhere in the repository, which is the open

  purchase on it, but saying "the build is green" here would be a claim this branch did not earn.
